Case File — UK Online Retail II · Chapters Three & Four

Does a fully rotatable, interactive visualization change what you find?

Twenty exhibits from a documented forensic analysis of 5,852 customers — each one built, rotated, and cross-checked against SQL before being called a finding.

Investigator: Cherrie Cosby
Opened: Jul 6, 2026
Chapter Three closed: Jul 16, 2026
Every exhibit below traces back to a numbered SQL query in the project's investigation log. Rotate them. Hover the points. Look for what a flat chart would hide. None of what's shown here was accepted on the strength of the visual alone — each finding was independently confirmed against the underlying data before it made it into this log.

Q98Log-scaled

RFM cube — log-scaled

The finding: no customer past ~300–400 days of silence ever reaches high spend. Recency and value travel together — there's no hidden population of lapsed high-value customers. Revised by Query 105 (recency_monetary_funnel_fixed_bucket_check) and Query 106 (recency_monetary_funnel_quartile_crosstab), run July 18, 2026 to confirm this chart-rotation finding in SQL for the first time: the absolute claim did not hold. Revised statement — average customer spend does decline steadily as recency increases, but a small, real population of high-value dormant customers exists at every recency level (61 of 1,463 top-quartile spenders also sit in the most-lapsed recency quartile). The funnel governs the typical customer; it does not govern the tail.

Q135–136Colored — Attempts

Never Converted — colored by attempt count

Same population, backdrop colored by order-attempt count (log-scaled). This exhibit prompted a real, SQL-confirmed finding — attempt count is tenure-driven, not random (Query 135) — and then produced a wrong specific reading of its own: a rotation appeared to show the high-attempt population peaking at 250–350 days recency; Query 136 checked this directly and found it was actually 94% concentrated under 100 days, a misread of several hundred days caused by overplotting at a dense viewing angle. Kept in the gallery deliberately, correction included, as the clearest example in this project of the standing rule earning its keep: a rotation can be trusted to flag something worth checking, not to report its own specifics correctly.
